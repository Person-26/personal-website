How this page is drawn

Every colour here is an electric field. The page is a flat slice z = z0, w = w0 through a standing electromagnetic wave in a four-dimensional box, and each pixel's red, green and blue (minus the background) are Ex, Ey and Ez there. The fourth component, Ew, is not shown. Its top and bottom edges are perfect conductors.

Nothing is drawn directly. The top and bottom of the screen are perfect conductors, and the hidden directions open out to far field, so the slice is a waveguide running left to right. The only inputs are drives at a single frequency on its two open ends, the left and right edges of the screen. Each colour component rides its own carrier with its own small momentum in the hidden directions, which lets Ew take up whatever Gauss's law asks for. That leaves the three visible components free, so the drives can make the slice show any colours: this page.

When you scroll, the field moves with the page, so text already formed slides along unchanged until it meets the top or bottom wall. There, as at any conductor, the components along the wall (Ex and Ez, red and blue) go to zero and only Ey reaches it. Meanwhile the drives change to hold what the move brings into view, and the change has to come in from the sides. The drives time every wave so that they all fall into step four seconds later. Until then you see them on their way: entering from the left and right, crossing the screen, bouncing off the top and bottom and interfering, until they focus into the new text. The hidden momentum works like a mass and differs by colour, so red, green and blue spread differently on the way and only line up once the page has settled. The simulation runs on a grid coarser than your screen, so once an area visibly matches the page it is forming (judged by comparing the two images, not the field), it slowly fades over to the same page drawn at full resolution, and fades back if new waves come through.
