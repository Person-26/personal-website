How this page is drawn

Every colour here is an electric field. The page is the flat slice z = 0 through an electromagnetic wave in three dimensions, and each pixel's red, green and blue (minus the background) are Ex, Ey and Ez there.

Nothing is drawn directly. The top and bottom of the screen are perfect conductors, the left and right ends are open, and space stays open above and below the slice. The light is ordinary light: massless, every wave moving at c, and every wave transverse, so Gauss's law holds exactly. The waves come in from the far field, through the left and right ends and from out of the plane, timed so that they all fall into step on the slice four seconds after they start. A wave tilted out of the slice can carry what a flat one cannot: the field along its own direction of travel in the plane, and Ez. So those parts of the colour arrive on steeper paths and only sharpen in close to the moment the page forms.

When you scroll, the field moves with the page, so text already formed slides along unchanged until it meets the top or bottom wall. There, as at any conductor, the components along the wall (Ex and Ez, red and blue) go to zero and only Ey reaches it. But the waves were held in step where the text used to be. When the input switches to the page as it now is, the old field is let go and runs on freely: it spreads out of the slice and out through the open ends, the time mirror of how it arrived. Meanwhile the new page comes in the same way, and until it focuses you see the waves on their way, crossing the screen, bouncing off the top and bottom and interfering. The simulation runs on a grid coarser than your screen, so once an area visibly matches the page it is forming (judged by comparing the two images, not the field), it slowly fades over to the same page drawn at full resolution, and fades back if new waves come through.
